Privacy

Games Drawer is a small, free site for playing games with family and friends. There are no accounts, no ads and no trackers, and we never sell anything about you. This is everything it keeps, and for how long.

What you type

  • Your name. Everyone at your table sees it. Your browser remembers it for next time; our server forgets it when the table closes.
  • A name on an invite. If you make an invite for someone by name, the server keeps both names with the table until it closes.
  • Chat messages. Everyone at the table sees them. The server keeps the last 10 while the table is open, so someone who joins late can catch up, and erases them when it closes.
  • A reported message. If someone taps Report on a message, that message and the sender’s name are saved so it can be looked at. It’s deleted after 30 days.

Your voice

  • The mic is only used once you turn it on.
  • Your voice goes straight to the others at your table, encrypted, or through our own relay server when a direct connection won’t work. It’s never recorded or saved.
  • To connect directly, the devices at your table learn each other’s internet address. With your mic off, they don’t.

Your own photos

  • If you add a photo for a jigsaw, your phone or computer makes it smaller first. That leaves its location, date and camera details behind on your device.
  • Before anyone sees it, it’s checked automatically for nudity, violence and the like by Amazon Rekognition, part of Amazon Web Services. No person looks at it, and we’ve told Amazon not to keep it or use it for anything else. A photo that doesn’t pass is thrown away.
  • Only the people at your table can see it. It’s kept only in our server’s memory, never saved, and it’s deleted when the table closes. Puzzles of your own photos aren’t saved either.

What the site keeps to work

  • In your browser: your name, a random number that gives you your seat back if you drop out, another for this device, the table you’re at (so reloading brings you back), when the page last reloaded itself for an update, your list of saved puzzles, and your sound and magnifier settings. No cookies. Clearing your browser’s data for this site removes them.
  • The device number lets us count how many devices play, rather than every tab or visit. It’s random, says nothing about you, and is only ever written down scrambled.
  • Saved puzzles: kept on our server so anyone at the table can carry on later. Just the picture and where the pieces are, no names. A finished puzzle is deleted, and a puzzle nobody has played for 30 days is deleted.
  • Logs: what happens in the games (a table opened, a game played, for how long) under scrambled numbers instead of your name, and how many chat messages were sent, not what they said. When a photo is added or turned away, they note that (and what kind of thing the check found), never the photo itself. They help us see what people enjoy and fix problems, and only the people who run the site see them. Your internet address is kept in our servers’ memory while you’re connected and for up to about an hour after, to stop abuse like too many tries, and these logs only ever have it scrambled. Logs are deleted after 30 days.
  • Our servers’ own logs: the voice relay and the web server keep short technical notes, which can include internet addresses. They’re deleted after 30 days too.

Who else is involved

Only Amazon Web Services, which runs the computers the site is on, in the United States, and checks the photos people add for puzzles. The fonts and pictures come from the site itself, so your browser doesn’t contact anyone else.

Children

Games Drawer isn’t meant for children under 13 on their own. Kids are welcome to play alongside a parent or grandparent.

House rules

Games Drawer is for playing games and talking with people you know. A few rules keep every table friendly.

  • Be kind. No bullying, threats, hate, or anything you wouldn’t say at a family dinner.
  • Invite people you know. Anyone with your table’s link or code can join it, so share it only with them. You can always see who’s at a table before you join.
  • Kids under 13 must play with a grown-up at their table.
  • Only add photos you’re allowed to share, like your own, and keep them family friendly. Photos are checked automatically, and ones with nudity, violence or hate symbols are turned away.
  • Mute or report. Tap someone’s message to mute them just for you, or to report it. In the Menu you can mute someone’s voice just for you, or leave the table.
  • Chat has a swearing filter. The people at a table can choose how strict it is, in the Menu.
  • Chats don’t last. Messages are erased when the table closes. A reported message is kept for 30 days so it can be looked at.

The fine print

  • Games Drawer is free and comes as it is. We try to keep it running and working well, but can’t promise it always will, and we can’t be responsible for what other people say or do at a table.
  • We may remove a message, close a table, or keep someone out if they break these rules.
  • Using the site means you agree to these rules and to the privacy page.

How to play

On a keyboard: Enter for messages, M for sound, F for full screen, Esc for the menu.

Invite someone

Send them a link. When they open it, they come straight to this table.

With a name, it’s filled in for them, so they just tap Join.

Or tell them the table code:

Play full screen

This browser can’t hide its bars. Instead, add Games Drawer to your home screen: it opens full screen, like an app.

  • iPhone (Safari): tap the Share button (the square with an arrow), then Add to Home Screen, then Add.
  • Android (Chrome): tap ⋮ at the top right, then Add to Home screen (or Install app).

Then open Games Drawer from its icon on your home screen.

Still playing?

Nobody has played or talked for a while, so this table closes in a minute.

Your mic is blocked

The browser was told not to let Games Drawer use the microphone. To allow it:

  • iPhone or iPad: tap aA at the left of the address bar, then Website Settings, then set Microphone to Allow.
  • Android (Chrome): tap the icon at the left of the address bar, then Permissions, and turn on Microphone.
  • Computer: click the icon at the left of the address bar and allow the Microphone.

Then tap Try again.
